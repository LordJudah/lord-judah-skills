# Worked Examples — Calibration Corpus

Two full transmutations from real viral threads. Use these to calibrate voice and to audit new outputs against a known standard.

---

## Example 1 — Bootstrapped Business Story Thread

**Source:** @jspujji thread — "100 days ago, I co-founded a business. Totally bootstrapped. Today, it's doing $50K in MRR growing 50% per month." Structure: story → three lessons → CTA.

**Source lessons:** (1) Just start — bias to action. (2) Leverage your unfair advantages — knowledge + network. (3) People/partners matter — trust and self-awareness made it real.

**Transmuted output:**

> Fifty thousand a month in a hundred days. The chapter was written before the market could finish its sentence.
>
> Speed can't be debated into existence.
> It has to be embodied.
> The fire was lit before the excuse could open its mouth.
>
> 1. **Start while it's unclear.** Their model would have predicted the future; instead they moved and made one. Nothing moves until you do — and motion is the only teacher that grades in real time.
>
> 2. **Stand on your unfair advantage.** Years of pattern recognition and a network built on trust formed the foundation no competitor could copy. Your lived archive is equity — don't apologize for it. Steward it.
>
> 3. **Find the one who carries half the vision with you.** The idea was a page until the right partner walked in. Someone who learns in public, holds standards, stays honest. A vision without a vessel is just a document.

**Verification:** 3 source lessons → 3 steps, in order · hook preserved the numbers (100 days, $50K) · no story details leak (the daycare, the hedge fund) · closing aphorism original.

## Example 2 — "Grow to 100K With One Content System" (representative tactic thread)

**Source:** Six-step cold-growth system — niche, batching, repurposing, analytics, engagement, own products; hustle-language throughout.

**Transmuted output:**

> They told you to chase the algorithm. I'm telling you to build an archive.
>
> Trends expire. Archives endure.
> The machine is not the mission —
> the mission is the transmission.
> Build the channel.
> Then feed the people.
>
> 1. Claim one frequency. A single lane, saturated — not ten topics pleading for ten crowds, but one truth, transmitted deep. Clarity is freedom: you stop competing for attention and start commanding it.
>
> 2. Create in seasons, not in moods. Ten pieces in one sitting beats one piece on a whim; your calendar answers to you, not to the feed's appetite. The artist who sets the session owns the session.
>
> 3. Let nothing die once. One transmission becomes many signals — video to words, words to reel, reel to letter. The archive is your amplifier; repetition with intention is legacy work.
>
> 4. Read the numbers as feedback, not judgment. Keep what resonates; release what's noise. Data tells you which truths landed — you decide why they landed.
>
> 5. Give before you post. Fifteen real minutes of engagement before your own drop — speak into somebody else's work with substance. Community is the algorithm that never changes; you're gathering a people, not farming attention.
>
> 6. Own your shelf. Sell what's yours; collect what you create. Your audience isn't a metric — it's a people, and authorship is the one equity no platform can take.

**Verification:** 6 steps → 6 steps · every number preserved (10 pieces, 15 minutes) · zero verbatim source phrases · one reframe per step · closer invitational.

## Calibration Notes

- Hooks preserved the **proof numbers** from both sources — numbers are credibility.
- All source-author personal facts (companies, customers, personal history) were either generalized ("their model," "a build") or dropped.
- Settings changed where the strategy allowed (quarterly batching vs "weekly") — but only in ways that strengthen the teaching, never in ways that lose the tactic.
- Sign-off lines are original aphorisms — never borrowed closers.
