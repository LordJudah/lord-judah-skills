# Hip-Hop Workshop Outreach

## Overview

Use this skill when a user wants to turn Lord Judah’s workshop vision into a polished proposal package and institution-specific outreach campaign. This skill is built for educational, nonprofit, cultural, and community-facing opportunities, especially when the work includes proposal writing, budget packaging, contact targeting, email personalization, and follow-up strategy.

Treat the work as both **program design** and **relationship-building**. The proposal must read as artistically credible, operationally realistic, and culturally grounded. The outreach must feel personal, concise, and institution-aware rather than generic.

## Core Positioning

Frame Lord Judah as a **creative director, hip-hop artist, producer, and teaching artist** whose work blends music, storytelling, performance, digital production, mindfulness, and community empowerment.

Use these recurring credibility anchors when relevant:

| Element | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Prior institutional connection | Mention that Lord Judah previously instructed at the Music Conservatory of Westchester under Dr. Aaron Flagg when that history strengthens trust or continuity. |
| Performance credibility | Mention that his hip-hop ensemble performed at Zankel Hall at Carnegie Hall when institutional audiences need proof of artistic excellence. |
| Teaching continuity | State that he has continued teaching hip-hop production and performance since that period, showing long-term commitment and practice. |
| Brand ecosystem | Position **www.judahblessent.com** as the bio/history site and **www.trueheartacademy.com** as the education and impact platform. |

Use both official websites in outreach materials unless the user explicitly requests a shorter signature.

## Default Workshop Configuration

Start from this default offer unless the user requests a different model.

| Category | Default configuration |
|---|---|
| Audience | Middle school through adult |
| Cohort model | Separate middle school and adult cohorts when feasible |
| Session length | 2 hours per class |
| Production platform | Soundtrap |
| Participant device plan | Participants bring laptops unless the host provides them |
| Core equipment | Smart Board or large display, USB microphones, headphones, stable internet |
| Recording environment | Quiet or acoustically isolated room suitable for recording |
| Instructional approach | Culturally responsive, hands-on, project-based, creativity-centered |

If the target institution cannot support a full production lab, adapt the scope without abandoning the workshop. For example, shift emphasis toward appreciation, writing, storytelling, rhythm, performance, and demonstration-based production.

## Workflow

Follow this sequence unless the user gives a narrower request.

1. **Clarify the opportunity type.** Determine whether the task is for a conservatory, school, library, nonprofit, youth center, or community arts venue.
2. **Define the program shape.** Confirm audience, duration, format, tech readiness, and whether the institution needs a pilot, residency, one-off workshop, or series.
3. **Build or adapt the proposal package.** Prepare the proposal narrative, workshop structure, and budget sheet.
4. **Research the institution and contact path.** Identify the best department, decision-maker, supporting contacts, and context for personalization.
5. **Write the outreach email.** Personalize the message to the institution’s mission, community, and likely program priorities.
6. **Check the signature block.** Include email, phone, both websites, and any relevant credibility language.
7. **Prepare attachments.** Attach the proposal and budget in clean, send-ready formats.
8. **Send or stage follow-up.** If authorized, send the email. Otherwise, prepare a review-ready draft. Track what has already been sent to avoid duplication.

## Proposal Writing Guidance

Write proposals in an institutional tone that still reflects cultural authenticity and artistic depth. The proposal should show that hip-hop is being presented as a rigorous art form, a technology-forward learning environment, and a vehicle for self-expression, confidence, and community impact.

Anchor the proposal around three layers of value.

| Layer | What the proposal should communicate |
|---|---|
| Artistic value | Hip-hop is taught as music, poetry, rhythm, storytelling, production, and performance. |
| Educational value | Participants gain digital production skills, collaboration habits, listening skills, and creative confidence. |
| Community value | The workshop supports identity, expression, inclusion, and meaningful participation. |

When designing curriculum language, keep the middle school version more scaffolded and confidence-building, while allowing the adult version to be more reflective, technical, and discussion-rich.

## Outreach Email Guidance

Every outreach email should do four things in a compact flow: establish identity, connect the offer to the institution, summarize the workshop clearly, and invite a next conversation.

Use a structure like this:

| Section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Opening | Introduce Lord Judah and the reason for reaching out |
| Credibility bridge | Reference MCW, Carnegie Hall, long-term teaching practice, or relevant lived experience when helpful |
| Program summary | Explain audience, format, outcomes, and tools in plain language |
| Logistics snapshot | Mention Soundtrap, laptops, microphones, headphones, display, and room needs |
| Call to action | Invite discussion, adaptation, pilot planning, or next steps |

Personalize the message to the target. A conservatory email can emphasize rigor and music technology. A library email can emphasize accessibility, digital literacy, public programming, and community engagement. A nonprofit or family center email can emphasize empowerment, youth development, healing, and voice.

## Adaptation Rules

When reusing this strategy for a new institution, decide what must stay fixed and what should shift.

| Keep consistent | Adapt to fit the target |
|---|---|
| Lord Judah’s identity and credibility | Institutional name, recipient, mission alignment, subject line |
| Workshop seriousness and cultural legitimacy | Age range emphasis, public-facing tone, cohort structure |
| Soundtrap-centered production model | Technical expectations based on host capacity |
| Need for a clear proposal and budget | Vocabulary that fits school, library, nonprofit, or arts language |
| Both websites and contact details | Specific call to action and next-step framing |

Do not let personalization distort the underlying offer. The adaptation should feel tailored, not reinvented.

## Follow-Up and Recordkeeping

Track which institutions have already been contacted, who was contacted, what attachments were sent, and whether any version used an outdated signature or missing website. If the user later adds new identity details, update the master signature standard and ask whether previously contacted venues should receive a refresh, follow-up, or corrected resend.

When follow-up is needed, use one of three approaches:

| Situation | Best move |
|---|---|
| Initial email received no response | Send a concise follow-up that references the earlier message and reaffirms fit |
| Materials changed meaningfully | Resend or forward an updated version with a brief explanation |
| Institution showed interest | Reply with proposed next steps, scheduling options, or adaptation notes |
